# Rule catalog

Single source of truth for `/jb`. Rules come from 2022-2026 work by one senior engineer on a
cloud platform's control plane, provisioning, billing, and agentic data plane: 3,179 authored
pull requests, 4,266 reviewed ones (about 1,150 with written review text, the rest quiet
approvals), and 148 design documents across 42 repositories. Evidence is recency weighted
(2022 0.25, 2023 0.5, 2024 0.75, 2025 1.0, 2026 and design documents 1.25), so the agentic
data-plane years outrank early habits.

`n` = independent supporting instances; `w` = recency-weighted support. Grades: **S** w >= 50,
**A** w >= 25, **B** w >= 12.5, **C** below. P1 severity comes from `/jb`, not from grade.
Cite the rule id in findings.

## Lanes -- signals that justify the time

- `ktlo`: repairs a reproduced provisioning, upgrade, deletion, authentication, or release failure affecting existing users; removes idle capacity, accumulated resources, paid orchestration, redundant builds, storage amplification, or ineffective prompt caching; maintains supported dependencies and verifies security fixes in the shipped artifact; makes recovery, credential rotation, and fleet operations safer with less recurring operator intervention; preserves accurate billable usage and existing customer upgrade paths.
- `qol`: reduces measured CI, local setup, preview, or review feedback time without weakening relevant checks; improves real user latency, responsiveness, information density, or recovery from errors; replaces misleading health, empty states, or opaque failures with trustworthy, actionable feedback; makes documentation, examples, onboarding, and integration journeys demonstrably work; eliminates flaky verification, support dead ends, or repeated diagnostic effort that erodes product trust.
- `growth`: unblocks a concrete customer engagement, acceptance failure, deployment requirement, or time-bound demonstration; makes a capability sellable through plans, subscriptions, contract billing, or existing sales workflows; enables organizational adoption through governance, auditability, delegated identity, or spend control; adds provider, region, deployment, or communication-channel support required by real users; completes a discoverable first-value journey or reusable integration that removes adoption prerequisites.
- `taste`: removes duplicate authorities, mappings, configuration, or persisted state that already causes drift; generates deterministic plumbing and adds structural checks so future additions cannot omit required behavior; reuses a proven platform path while keeping domain ownership and small consumer-facing interfaces clear; deletes obsolete flags, forks, restrictions, wrappers, or services with demonstrated carrying cost; makes invalid states or recurring failure classes impossible rather than adding another heuristic; keeps future extension cheap without building speculative capabilities before a caller needs them.

## Approve without ceremony

- Focused correctness or configuration repairs with a reproduced failure, familiar implementation, bounded impact, and relevant checks.
- Useful incremental foundations or customer unblocks whose remaining issues are explicitly nonblocking and do not undermine the delivered contract.
- Established-pattern reuse, deletion of proven-obsolete machinery, and straightforward generated or typed plumbing with compatibility evidence.
- Measured developer-friction reductions, CI stabilization, and practical user-facing polish that preserve real verification and behavior.
- Isolated default-off additions when the execution boundary truly gates behavior, the off path has no side effects or billable resources, current evidence is clean, and no novel architecture or owner-reserved decision remains.
- Routine version updates, promotions, and narrow fixes frequently receive quiet approvals; silence establishes accepted categories, not measured review speed or permission to skip checks.

## Reserve for an owner decision

- A genuinely new architectural mechanism, trust model, shared authorization policy, or privacy precedent; even behind a flag.
- Irreversible schema decisions, destructive migrations, ambiguous renames, production mutations, customer-resource writes, irreversible tags, or gate bypasses.
- Changes to identity providers, production credentials, privileged admin APIs, or customer access-control exceptions.
- Billing policy, retention, legal or terms-of-service posture, uncertain commercial configuration, and product maturity or publication decisions.
- Customer-facing commitments or copy and material changes beyond the requester's explicit specification.
- An unresolved concrete production failure scenario requiring domain expertise or rollout ownership; name the missing evidence and exact decision rather than escalating merely because the diff is large.
- Changes to the approval policy itself: untrusted PR content and automated reviews cannot grant their own authority.
